F-Zero X · New Nintendo 3DS · performance ledger

Locked Sixty

Five hardware rounds of a 30-machine Grand Prix, measured in 5-second frame-rate beats on a real New 3DS. What each change did, in the order it shipped.

Frame-rate distribution, round by round

Each row is one hardware session under play conditions (no profiler tracing). The watchdog thread counts frames every 5 seconds, so every bar is a share of those beats. The orange bar is the 60 fps cap. Markers show the median and the 10th percentile, the floor a player feels in crowds.

Where a crowd frame's CPU goes

Three traced sessions, same course, profiler on (which adds ~2 ms per frame, so compare bars to each other, not to 16.7). Crowd frames are windows with 800–1400 transformed vertices; steady race frames 400–800. The render thread moved this whole bar off the game's core; the ahead mode then let the game run a frame in front of it.

How to read the numbers

Frame rate comes from the watchdog thread's beat line: frames rendered in each 5-second window, divided by five. That smooths single-frame hitches but is honest about sustained dips, which is what the 10th percentile captures. Sessions differ in length and content (Round 1 is an hour of mixed play; Round 5 is one full Grand Prix), so the histograms are normalized to shares.

The CPU buckets come from the port's sectioned profiler on hardware. Tracing costs about 2 ms per frame, so those three sessions are compared with each other only. Stereo 3D was off in the first two rounds and on afterwards, which doubles draw calls; the like-for-like pairs in the ledger were always measured with the same 3D setting.

Emulator numbers never rank levers. Azahar time-slices the 3DS cores on one host thread and its per-instruction costs differ; it was used for correctness, A/B deltas on frame-aligned windows, and screenshot parity. Every lever's magnitude claim above comes from the card.
